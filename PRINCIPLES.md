# Principles

*What we do about what we value. Version 0.2, a draft for public comment. The [manifesto](MANIFESTO.md) is the short text people sign; this page is the rulebook behind it. Changes are listed in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md).*

Every principle here is a promise we can be held to.

## We give one day

The unit is one day. Whatever your cadence, the pledge is counted in whole days and reported in public. Organizations report and renew every year. Individuals are counted by the days they log. One day a month for years beats one heroic weekend.

If your day comes from an employer's time, your employer pledges with you and authorizes the license. If it comes from your own time, say so when you sign.

## We build in the open

Everything we make is open source, open data, a public good. Code is released under an OSI-approved license, data and content under an open license, and every mission aims to meet the Digital Public Goods Standard. Everyone may use it, forever. The licenses we use cannot be revoked, and nothing is ever relicensed to a non-open license.

Names, marks, and repositories will be held by a non-profit home the movement chooses, never by a member company. Until then they are held by the founder, through the movement's GitHub organization, as [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) records.

## We choose problems, not projects

Missions are selected for the suffering they can reduce, not the attention they attract. People who live with the problem, whether patients, caregivers, displaced people, or clinicians on the ground, sit on every mission's council. They hold a blocking vote on the mission's scope, its use of data, and any release to the people it serves. A block is resolved by the Steering Circle within 30 days.

## We finish things

Every mission has a steward, a backlog, and a definition of done. The steward is a named person accountable for the backlog, the definition of done, and a quarterly report. A mission with no steward for 60 days is paused. Backlogs are groomed so that a newcomer can complete something meaningful in a single day, and a task that cannot be finished in a day is split until it can. Enthusiasm is not a plan. A day that ends without something merged, reviewed, tested, or learned was a day we owe.

## We measure honestly

Progress is published. Failure is published too, in the same place, in the same font. Every mission keeps a public log of what worked, what didn't, and what changed as a result. Three things are published on a schedule: a quarterly report from each mission, an annual post from each organization, and quarterly accounts from the movement.

## We do no harm by design

Privacy, safety, and consent come before speed. Health data is handled to GDPR Article 9 standards wherever the person lives, with HIPAA Security Rule safeguards wherever protected health information is touched. Every mission states which medical-device rules apply to what it ships, and nothing that a dosing decision could depend on ships without saying so.

## We are impartial

We are non-partisan and non-sectarian. We choose conflict work by need, not by side. Before any mission touches conflict data we ask who it could harm, and protecting people from harm caused by our own work comes before anything else we might achieve.

## Time is the pledge; money is fuel

Cash is welcome. It funds stewards, infrastructure, and compute, and never a member company's own products. No amount of money substitutes for showing up.

## We maintain what we make

A share of every mission's days, one in four by default, goes to keeping earlier work alive: security, dependencies, documentation, support.

## We show up

We are not the majority. We may never be. We are a fraction, on purpose: not everyone will join, and a fraction that shows up, every time, is what turns intention into change.
