## About (2,000 characters at most; this is about 1,250)

Most of the world's technical talent spends every working hour on problems that pay. Disease,
hunger, war, exclusion: the problems that matter most are worked on by too few, with too little,
for too short a time.

One Day for Humanity is a commitment by people and organizations to give a fixed, recurring
share of their working time to humanity's hardest problems, and to give the results away.

The unit is one day. Every week, every month, every quarter, or every year: the cadence is
yours, the commitment is public, and every cadence counts. The time goes into missions:
open-source efforts with a named steward, a backlog cut into day-sized tasks, and a definition
of done. Everything built there is a public good. Everyone may use it, forever.

Progress is published. Failure is published too, in the same place, in the same font.

We are pre-launch. The manifesto is a draft, open for comment and open to signatures. Our
proposed founding mission is open diabetes technology, feeding the projects that several
thousand people already rely on, and it becomes a mission the day it has a steward.

Read the manifesto and sign it at onedayforhumanity.org.

## First post, from the page

Today we are opening One Day for Humanity for public comment.

The idea is small on purpose. People and organizations give one day, every week, month, quarter,
or year, to humanity's hardest problems, and give the results away. The day is counted, kept,
and reported in public. The work goes into open-source missions with a steward and a backlog
anyone can pick up.

Nothing is launched yet. The manifesto is a draft, the founding mission is a proposal, and the
first steward has not stepped forward. That is why we are publishing now rather than later: we
would rather be argued with than admired.

Read it, sign it, or tell us what is wrong with it: onedayforhumanity.org

## First post, from you

I have spent most of my working life on problems that pay. I do not think that is because I do
not care, and I do not think it is true of the people I have worked with either. Others have
built doors before us, and most of the time given through them is scattered, unreported, and
gone when the enthusiasm goes.

So I am trying a door that stays open. One Day for Humanity asks people and organizations for
one day, on whatever cadence they can keep, given to humanity's hardest problems in the open,
with the results given away. Not a hackathon. Not a donation. A recurring day, counted and
reported.

It is a draft. The manifesto is open for comment, the founding mission is a proposal, and I am
looking for the first steward. If you have ever wanted to give a day and not known where, read
it: onedayforhumanity.org

## A note on tone

No exclamation marks, no emoji, no hashtag walls. If you want hashtags, two at the end are
enough: #opensource #digitalpublicgoods. The page and the posts should read like the
manifesto, because they are the manifesto's front door.
